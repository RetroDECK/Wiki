## A Statement of Love

When we the RetroDECK Team started this project many years ago, we had a bold vision: to create our dream retro gaming experience in an *all-in-one Retro Gaming Platform* called RetroDECK.

We wanted to build a platform that reflects our love for the entire retro gaming space, not just the most well-known games and consoles, but the countless communities, projects and experiences that keep retro gaming alive.

Retro gaming is much bigger than emulation alone. It includes:

- Engine reimplementations such as **UZDoom** and other source ports.
- Complete game engines such as **Solarus** and **Open Surge** that keep the spirit of specific retro game genres alive.
- Emulators and multi-system emulation projects.
- Fantasy consoles such as **PICO-8**, alongside modern interpretations of retro hardware such as the **GameTank** and **Commander X16**.
- Dedicated pinball communities that keep their pinball halls lit.
- MUD communities that continue to keep decades-old servers alive.
- Retro computer enthusiasts and longtime veterans who continue to create new software, games and hardware for the systems they love, year after year.
- Mods, standalone games, fan projects, romhacks and countless other creations.

Behind every one of these projects is a community with its own history, passion and memories.

There is an incredible amount of love for specific games, hardware, software and the experiences people grew up with. Many of these communities are small or easy to overlook, but they are an important part of what makes retro gaming so special.

### Bringing More Into the Light

This update represents our desire to bring more of these communities into the light.

We want RetroDECK to make it easier to discover and launch the things people have created, whether that means:

- Playing a mod or adding a romhack.
- Launching a standalone game.
- Running a fan project built with a dedicated game engine.
- Connecting to a MUD server.
- Exploring a niche platform or community.

Our goal is not to define what retro gaming *should* be.

Our goal is to make more of it **accessible**.

This update is also a symbol of what we have been working toward for many years.

It represents the ideas we had when we started, the communities we wanted to support and what we have been able to accomplish together.

We hope it shows what RetroDECK can become when we bring these different parts of the retro gaming world together in one accessible platform. Offering a fresh perspective on what a unified retro gaming experience can be.

To our community and former RetroDECK Team members: 

**We love you and we are incredibly grateful for your support!**

And to everyone across the retro gaming space, from the largest projects to the smallest niche communities:

**We love you!**

This update is our way of celebrating that shared passion with an **Everything Explosion of Love**.

And don't worry, we have a set of ideas and improvements planned before we call it a RetroDECK 1.0 and development is progressing toward that milestone.

Additional content and enhancements are also planned for releases after 1.0, so don't worry we have no plans to go away anytime soon and have a lot more crazy ideas.

**Now, on with the show!**

## RetroDECK Framework

<img src="../../../wiki_icons/retrodeck/icon-framework.svg" width="75" alt="RetroDECK Framework Logo">

 **Removed the b suffix from versioning:** Following community feedback (very angry people on Lemmy that hates letters with a burning passion), we have dropped the `b` suffix from version numbers, so *let it `b` gone*.
- **Component Isolation:** Component-specific logic is now contained within its respective module and runs in a dedicated subsandbox on launch. Central functions that were specific to individual components have also been moved out of the main project and isolated within the component they belong to.
- **ES-DE JSON Translation:** Each component now defines its own ES-DE capabilities and configuration, replacing ES-DE's static XML lists: `es_find_rules.xml` and `es_systems.xml` and manual upkeep.
- **Startup / Shutdown Hooks:** Components can execute functions on `startup` and `shutdown` through `component_functions.sh`.
- **Unified Component Files:** Merged `component_update.sh` and `component_prepare.sh` into `component_functions.sh`.
- **Dynamic Component Loading:** Components are discovered and loaded dynamically at boot, automatically registering their tools, functions and ES-DE logic. This allows RetroDECK to detect available components, their capabilities and how they should run on each boot, while enabling support for components beyond the shipped internal components.
- **First Install:** Updated and changed first-install prompts to be more relevant and global, rather than component-specific.
- **Moved Global Functions to Framework:** Moved shared functions from the main application into the Framework component, including helper-file and folder-icon logic.
- **Updated Licences Files:** Condensed and updated the licences files to include all new components and features.

### Gargoyle

<img src="../../../wiki_images/logos/gargoyle-logo.webp" width="75" alt="Gargoyle logo">

**Gargoyle** is a multi-engine interpreter for classic text adventures and interactive fiction, supporting a wide range of formats through a unified interface.

It provides a modern interface for playing text-based adventures and other interactive-fiction games.

RetroDECK supports Gargoyle through two systems:

- `roms/zmachine` - for Z-machine games.
- `roms/if` - A **new** a general-purpose system supporting all interactive-fiction formats compatible with Gargoyle, that goes beyond just Z-machine games. Opening up the wider world of interactive fiction within RetroDECK.
