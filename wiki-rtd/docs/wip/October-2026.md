## RetroDECK 0.11.0 - What Is in It So Far?

<img src="../../../icon-rd.svg" width="75" alt=""> 

Quite a lot.

The RetroDECK Team is passionate about retro gaming culture. This update aims to showcase the wide range of people communities and projects that make up the retro gaming space.

We want to use the RetroDECK platform to tell their stories and shine a light on tiny communities that provide significant value to retro gaming culture but may not yet be widely known. 

RetroDECK's core missions is to make retro gaming easy and accessible:

- **Easy to play** your own game backups.
- **Easy to experience** mods, ROM hacks, fan projects and other reinterpretations of classic games.
- **Easy to explore** vintage games and platforms you may never have experienced before.
- **Easy to discover** the people and communities that continue to preserve, reinterpret and expand retro gaming culture.

## Why the oddball systems? Retro Gaming Computers, Mudlet and Gargoyle - A shared history 

Mudlet, Gargoyle and retro-computing emulators such as KEGS and Oricutron all offer ways to explore the history of interactive storytelling and early gaming.

Interactive fiction games popular on these systems introduced a new way of experiencing stories, allowing players to influence events through their choices and actions. One of the defining early works was **Colossal Cave Adventure**, which helped establish the foundations of the adventure game genre. Later, **Zork** expanded on these ideas, offering a more detailed world and greater complexity.

The same era also saw the rise of branching narratives in print, maybe influenced by the popularity of interactive fiction games. By the late 1970s, *Choose Your Own Adventure* books were introducing readers to stories in which their decisions determined the direction of the narrative.

Another major milestone that changed gaming forever came in 1974, when Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson published **Dungeons & Dragons** (D&D). This was followed by the **Dungeons & Dragons Basic Set** and **Advanced Dungeons & Dragons** helping to establish the tabletop role-playing game (TRPG) genre.

Dungeons & Dragons helped popularise many conventions now associated with role-playing games, including:

- Character classes
- Character levels
- Experience points
- Exploratory storytelling and narrative choices
- Dungeon crawling
- And much more

The one of the roots of Dungeons & Dragons goes back to **Chainmail**, a medieval miniatures wargame published in 1971 by Gary Gygax and Jeff Perren. That drew on a long tradition of military simulation games, stretching all the way back to Prussian wargaming in 1780 and later the making of the Prussian game *Kriegsspiel* in the early 1820. 

This tradition continued through later wargames, including **Gettysburg** by Avalon Hill in 1958.

D&D also influenced the creation of many of the fantasy worlds we know today, including **Warhammer**, **Ultima** and **Warcraft**. 

In Japan, TRPG-inspired campaigns helped shape fantasy storytelling in manga and anime, for example: **Record of Lodoss War** that originated from a series of D&D-inspired tabletop role-playing sessions. These influences also helped shape Japanese RPGs like *Dragon Quest* and *Final Fantasy*, while also being inspired by Western computer RPGs such as *Ultima*.

By establishing many of the core mechanics, character archetypes and storytelling conventions of fantasy role-playing, D&D laid the groundwork for countless worlds that grew from the imagination of players around the tabletop that is felt to this day in modern video games and games of that era.

### Allowing you to experince the stories told

By incorporating a engine component like **Gargoyle**, an interactive fiction interpreter, it becomes possible to revisit many of these early works and experience the pioneering forms of storytelling that influenced an entire generation of game designers and players.

Building on this idea, a client component like **Mudlet** allows you to connect to MUD (Multi-User Dungeon) servers, representing a natural evolution of interactive fiction into shared, multiplayer experiences. 

- What if these stories could be experienced together with others? 
- Like a tabletop D&D campaign? 

A world where players can meet friends, form guilds and teams, participate in player-driven economies and explore vast, freely navigable worlds, all through text.

This vision transformed interactive fiction from a solitary experience into a shared adventure, where players could shape the world together through their actions and interactions. Many pioneers of the MUD era went on to work on commercial online games, including creating **Ultima Online** and creation of the MMORPG genre and influence the development of sandbox style games.

There is an inherent connection between these early multiplayer worlds and not only modern MMORPGs but also things like modded Minecraft servers, which face many of the same challenges that developers encountered in the early days of MUDs. 

- How do you create a functioning player-driven economy? 
- How do you establish ecological systems, meaningful resource management and sustainable communities? 
- And how do you encourage players to engage with one another in ways that go beyond the basic sandbox mechanics?

By connecting to an old MUD server, you can experience firsthand how these early virtual worlds worked... AND if you're lucky, perhaps even make a few friends along the way.

Or maybe you will find inspiration deep within the very caves of **Zork** or **Colossal Cave Adventure**, where the foundations of interactive storytelling and virtual world exploration first took shape.

## Why the oddball systems? Modding, Preservation and Fan Interpretations

The goal is simply to make the extensive range of community-created content more accessible for people to experience.

### Modding

This selection of components represents the modding and source port community:

- **dhewm 3** (DOOM 3)
- **ECWolf** (Wolfenstein 3D)
- **Ironwail** (Quake)
- **UZDoom** (DOOM) — replaces GZDoom
- **Delta Patcher** (.xdelta ROM hacks)

We want to simplify launching these games and their mods as separate entries in ES-DE, making their vast library of community-created content easier to discover and play. Thus we have introduced new custom parser formats (`.doom3`, `.wolf`, and `.quake`), following a similar concept as our existing `.doom` format.

While a software like Delta Patcher helps you patch rom hacks AKA mods to your disc based games. 

### Fan Interpretations

This selection of components represents the very niece fan interpretations community:

- **Open Surge** (Sonic-inspired 2D games)
- **Commander X16 Emulator** (Commander X16)
- **GameTankEmulator** (GameTank)

Just as Solarus provides an engine for creating classic Zelda-inspired games (Zelda-like), Open Surge does the same for classic Sonic-inspired games the Sonic-like.

To make launching these games in ES-DE easier, we've also created a custom parser format: `.surge`.

With RetroDECK 0.11.0, launching Open Surge games is now easier than using the official Open Surge Flatpak.

The GameTank and Commander X16 are both modern interpretations of 8-bit computing and gaming. The Commander X16 is inspired by classic home computers like the Commodore 64, while the GameTank is an 8-bit gaming console inspired by the Sega Master System and NES. 

Both projects offer real fan hardware as well as their own emulators.

### Preservation

This selection of components represents the game preservation community:

- **ScummVM** (Adventure games and RPGs)
- **Visual Pinball X** (Pinball simulation)
- **Flycast** (SEGA Dreamcast)

ScummVM is well known as an engine for playing classic point-and-click adventure games and role-playing games. Although we've always supported it through a RetroArch core, it was time to include the full application in RetroDECK and the same is true for Flycast.

Visual Pinball X is a pinball simulator that plays an important role in the retro gaming and pinball preservation communities. Enthusiasts use it to build their own interactive virtual pinball tables, recreate classic machines, and practice before tournaments.

The pinball scene has also experienced something of a renaissance in both the US and Europe, with Gen X and Millennials helping revive pinball halls and arcades.

New pinball machines are still being produced today by companies such as American manufacturer Stern Pinball and Swedish manufacturer Pinball Brothers, among others.

Since we've already included MAME as a major part of our arcade preservation efforts, it made sense to include the other side of arcade gaming as well: pinball machines.

# Why? Other?!

- **Xenia Edge** (Xbox 360)
- **Quake3e** (Quake III Arena)

Xbox 360 emulation has been at the top of our list for years, with community members repeatedly asking for it (aggressively). It's an important system for both Millennials and Gen Z. 

The Xbox 360, together with Xbox Live Arcade (XBLA), helped shape modern gaming by creating online communities and making indie games more accessible through titles such as *Braid* and *Castle Crashers*. It also connected people through online multiplayer in ways that console gaming had rarely seen before.

Even features we take for granted today, such as achievements, made Sony play catch-up with Microsoft throughout that entire generation.

Before the Xbox 360, this kind of interconnected gaming experience was largely associated with PC gamers, who enjoyed LAN parties and online multiplayer. Microsoft brought that connected experience to the console audience on a much larger scale.

That brings us to **Quake3e**, which represents the LAN party culture of the Millennials. As an enhanced client and engine for *Quake III Arena*, it makes it easy to play a variety of mods (we've also created a custom parser format: `.quake3`). However, it lacks proper controller support.

Just grab a mouse and keyboard but as the Millennials might say: *"Stop being such weaksauce! Just git gud scrub... don't be a n00blet forever!"*
